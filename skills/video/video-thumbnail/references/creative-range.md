# Creative range

Use when preparing the first thumbnail round, or reopening a repetitive round.

## Recognizable without repeating

Keep Dan's real likeness, readable type, and a clear promise. Those can connect
the channel without fixing the face position, expression, background, or props.
Start with what the viewer should understand visually, then choose the photo
and layout that convey it. Do not work backward from a familiar template.

Across a normal three-concept round, aim for visibly different silhouettes:
perhaps a face-led composition, an object-led metaphor, and an environment or
comparison. These are possibilities, not required slots. A no-face option is
useful only when the object can carry the idea and Dan has not requested his
face in every thumbnail.

Ask of each pair: if the words were hidden and colors removed, would the
position, scale, and relationship of the subjects still distinguish them?
Different template names, outfits, or alternate copies do not answer this test.

## Choose photos as part of the idea

The September 2026 library audit found 14 cutouts but 105 original images.
The originals already included crossed arms, side glances, and right-pointing
poses missing from the cutout picker. Recount the library when needed; these
numbers describe that audit, not a permanent inventory.

- Inspect original-photo picker pages across shoots, not just the first page.
- Shortlist by gesture and gaze: pointing, crossed arms, hand on chin, open
  hands, clasped hands, natural smile, or other poses actually present.
- Distinguish a different pose from another shirt in the same pose.
- Prefer less recently used suitable sources. Never claim a photo is unused
  just because it is absent from the cutout library; usage may be unknown.
- Full studio photos can convey a personal opinion more naturally than another
  detached reaction face. Use them directly when the setting helps.
- Use existing source photos before asking for another shoot. If something
  truly is missing, specify the shot: expression, hand position, gaze, crop,
  and free space. Do not invent an available asset.

New cutouts should preserve the source link in `cutouts/manifest.md`, along with
pose family, gaze direction, visible hands, outfit, and useful crop. Rebuild the
cutout picker after additions. Inspect edges at full size as needed.

## Keep a small decision record

Save `selection.md` alongside each round's HTML/PNGs. Keep it practical:

```markdown
Working title:
Promise and source: ContentOS brief / local file / explicit provisional assumption
Recent comparison set: paths or links, with selected/published/draft status
Repeated patterns to avoid this round:

| Option | Visual argument | Photo source | Pose/gaze | Face position/scale | Main object |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Recommendation and tradeoff:
Status: exploratory / awaiting choice / selected / verified published
Selected option and decision date:
Feedback worth carrying forward:
```

A future round can read the recent records and final art without rediscovering
photo usage. Unknown history stays unknown. Record outcomes or test results
only when supplied or verified; visual novelty alone is not evidence of clicks.

## Example: You Might Not Need Spec-Driven Development

The initial title-only exploration suggested a large spec stack beside a tiny
fix. Reading the ContentOS brief changed the direction: the actual video compares
four tools, demonstrates a Spring Store, and teaches ownership of the process.
This is why the brief comes before the template.

- Comparison: four named tool cards versus Dan's PRODUCT.md plan, with a small
  considering portrait. Possible text: `MY PLAN`.
- Result: the store as the main object with a natural smiling studio photo.
  Possible text: `I BUILT THIS`. Label prototype references in the decision
  record and replace them with current app captures before final delivery.
- Ownership: a confident crossed-arms portrait and a direct statement.
  Possible text: `OWN YOUR PROCESS`.

These share a promise while changing gesture, face scale/position, and visual
argument. They do not turn the conditional title into a claim that planning is
useless. The title remains a proposal until Dan selects it.
